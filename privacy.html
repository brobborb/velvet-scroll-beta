Privacy

Velvet Scroll website and browser extension · Updated 10 November 2026

In short: your books, reading positions and settings stay in your own browser. Velvet Scroll has no accounts, no advertising and no server that receives your library. The website counts visits anonymously, without cookies (see The website); the extensions don’t. The only information it sends anywhere is a book's title and author, and only when you ask it to look up a book's details.

What stays on your device

The books you add, their covers, your reading positions, shelves, book details and display settings are stored in your browser's own storage on your device. They are not uploaded to velvetscroll.net or anywhere else. Clearing your browser's site data, or removing the extension, deletes them.

Looking up book details

When you use Find details, or search from a book's Details window, Velvet Scroll sends that book's title and author to these public book databases, and downloads cover images from them:

  • Open Library (Internet Archive): openlibrary.org, covers.openlibrary.org
  • Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons: wikidata.org, commons.wikimedia.org, upload.wikimedia.org
  • Project Gutenberg, through the Gutendex search service: gutendex.com, gutenberg.org

No other information about you or your library is sent. In the extension, your browser asks your permission before it contacts these sites, and you can withdraw it at any time in your browser's extension settings. On the website, Velvet Scroll asks you once before the first lookup; to withdraw it, clear this site's data in your browser. Once allowed, books you add are looked up automatically, applying details only where the title and author both match. Those services handle requests under their own privacy policies.

On the website, browsers only let a page read another site's data if that site allows it. For the databases and cover images that don't, the request passes through Velvet Scroll's download service at fetch.velvetscroll.net (a Cloudflare Worker), which fetches it and passes it straight back. It doesn't store or log what passes through. Cloudflare handles the connection under its own privacy policy.

Adding books from a web address

If you use Add from URL, Velvet Scroll downloads the file from the address you enter, straight from your browser, like any other download. That site receives the request, including your IP address, under its own privacy policy. Nothing else is sent. The extension asks your permission before it contacts a site. On the website, books from the free libraries that don't let other websites download their files (such as Project Gutenberg and arXiv) come through fetch.velvetscroll.net, described above, which passes the file straight back without storing it.

Author bios

The Details window can show a short bio of a book's author. To find it, Velvet Scroll sends the author's name, and nothing else, to Wikidata (wikidata.org) and Wikipedia (wikipedia.org), the first time you open the details of a book by that author, or when you choose Look up on Wikipedia. In the browser extension this happens only after your browser has asked your permission. The bio is then kept on your device, shared by all that author's books, and included in your backups and Google Drive sync. You can edit or remove it, or write your own. Text from Wikipedia is available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licence and is shown with a link to its article.

Saving articles (extension only)

When you save a web page, the extension reads that page in your browser, keeps a clean copy of the article and stores it in your library on your device. It reads a page only when you choose Save this page, use the right-click option, or paste a link into Save article; for a pasted link, your browser asks your permission to contact that site. Pictures are copied into the saved article where the site allows; any that can't be copied are loaded from the original site when you read the article, just as if you visited it. Saved articles are never sent to velvetscroll.net or anyone else.

Dictionary (optional)

If you choose to download the dictionary, Velvet Scroll downloads it once (about 14 MB) from velvetscroll.net and keeps it in your browser’s storage. Words you look up are found on your device and are never sent anywhere. The More on Wiktionary link opens that word’s page on Wiktionary only if you click it. You can remove the dictionary at any time under Display.

Translating books (optional, experimental)

If you turn on Translate under Display, Velvet Scroll downloads Mozilla’s translation engine and a translation model once (about 30 MB) from velvetscroll.net and keeps them in your browser. Translation happens entirely on your device: the text of your books is never sent anywhere, and no translated copy is saved or shared. You can remove the downloaded files at any time under Display.

Read aloud

Read aloud uses the voices provided by your device and browser. Most are built into your device; some browsers also offer online voices, marked "online" in the voice list, which send the text being read to the browser maker's speech service. Choose a voice without "online" to keep reading aloud on your device.

Natural voices (optional)

If you choose to use a natural voice, Velvet Scroll downloads that voice (about 63 MB) from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), and a pronunciation dictionary (about 18 MB) from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net). On the website, the speech engine's code also comes from jsDelivr; the extension includes it. Like any download, these services receive the technical details of the request, such as your IP address. The voices are kept in your browser and run entirely on your device: the text being read is never sent anywhere. You can remove the downloads at any time under Display, in the reader. Details of the software and voice licences are on the licences page.

Recognising text in scanned PDFs (optional)

If you choose Recognise text for a scanned PDF, Velvet Scroll downloads English language data (about 3 MB) from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net); on the website, the text recogniser's code (about 4 MB) comes from jsDelivr too, while the extension includes it. As with any download, jsDelivr receives the technical details of the request, such as your IP address. The recognising happens entirely on your device: page images and the text found on them are never sent anywhere. The recognised text is stored in your browser with the book and is deleted when you remove the book.

Browser sync (extension only, optional)

If you turn on sync, your reading positions, shelves, book details and settings are saved to your browser's built-in sync (Firefox Sync or Chrome sync) so they follow you between your own computers. The books themselves are never synced. This data is handled by Mozilla or Google under their privacy policies. Sync is off unless you turn it on.

Google Drive sync (optional)

If you choose Connect Google Drive, Velvet Scroll keeps one small file, "Velvet Scroll sync.json", in your own Google Drive. It holds your reading positions, shelves, book details, highlights and notes, and settings, so they match on each device where you connect. The books themselves are never uploaded. Velvet Scroll asks Google only for permission to see and change files that Velvet Scroll created, so it can't see anything else in your Drive. The file goes straight between your browser and Google; it doesn't pass through any Velvet Scroll server, and Velvet Scroll doesn't keep a copy anywhere else. Google's sign-in gives your browser a short-lived access key, which is kept only in this browser. You can disconnect at any time from Backup in Velvet Scroll, remove access from your Google Account's security settings, and delete the file from your Drive. Google handles the file under its own privacy policy. Velvet Scroll's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Sync codes

A sync code is made on your device and encrypted with AES-256 using the passphrase you choose. It holds your reading positions, shelves, book details and settings, but no books. Velvet Scroll doesn't send it anywhere: you copy, share or scan it yourself. If you scan its QR code, your phone opens velvetscroll.net with the code in the address after the "#", a part of the address browsers don't send to the website, and the code still can't be read without the passphrase.

Backups

A backup is a file saved to your own device. Velvet Scroll doesn't send it anywhere. If you choose a password, the backup is encrypted with AES-256 before it's saved.

The website

velvetscroll.net is hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may record technical information such as IP addresses when pages are loaded. The website also loads fonts from Google Fonts, code libraries from cdnjs (Cloudflare), and, when you open a PDF, the PDF library's standard font and character-map files from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net). These services receive the same kind of technical request information; none of them receive your books. The extension includes its fonts and libraries, so it doesn't contact these services.

To see how many people visit, the website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics. When a page loads, it records the page’s address, the page you came from, your country, and the type of browser and device. It uses no cookies, doesn’t identify you and doesn’t follow you across other websites. It never sees your books or anything you read in Velvet Scroll. The extensions don’t include it. Cloudflare handles this data under its own privacy policy.

Velvet Scroll Plus

Velvet Scroll Plus is an optional one-time purchase. Its licence key is kept in your browser on your device and checked there, using a signature built into Velvet Scroll. Checking the key doesn't contact any server, and nothing about you or your library is sent anywhere when you use it. The key contains only the name it was issued to, the date and a random reference number.

Contributions

Contributions are optional and made directly through PayPal or Bitcoin. Velvet Scroll never sees or stores payment details. PayPal handles payments under its own privacy policy, and Bitcoin transactions are public on the Bitcoin network.
