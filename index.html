        Accessibility

        These settings apply everywhere: the library, the reader and every window.

        High contrast

        Colour vision

        Red–green suits protanopia and deuteranopia (the most common kinds); blue–yellow suits tritanopia. The accent, reading guide, word highlight and progress bars switch to colours that stay distinct, and selected buttons and warnings get ✓ and ⚠ marks so nothing relies on colour alone.

        Size

        Makes the buttons, menus and labels bigger and easier to tap. The book's own text size is under Display.

        Reading

        Switches the page to Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for low vision, and adds a little space between letters and words, which some readers with dyslexia find helpful. Turning it off puts your typeface back.

        Motion

        Appearance

        Colours for the library and panels. The reading page has its own colours under Display in the reader.

        Theme
        Accent colour
        Background

        Pick a light colour and it's used in the light theme; pick a dark one and it's used in the dark theme. Velvet Scroll makes a matching shade for the other theme, and adjusts the text and accent so they stay easy to read.

        Save an article

        Paste the address of an article, story or blog post. Velvet Scroll keeps a clean, readable copy in your library, with its pictures where the site allows.

        On a page you’re reading, you can also save it with the Velvet Scroll button in the browser toolbar, or by right-clicking the page.

        Add from a web address

        Paste a link to an ebook or PDF: EPUB, PDF, MOBI, AZW3, FB2, TXT or CBZ. Links to a book’s page on Project Gutenberg or arXiv work too, as do shared Dropbox, Google Drive and GitHub links. DRM-protected books can’t be added.

        Many sites don’t let other websites download their files. If a link doesn’t work here, download the file in your browser and use Add books, or use the Velvet Scroll browser extension, which can download from any site you allow.

        Natural voices

        Natural voices use Piper, an open-source speech system, to read aloud in a more lifelike voice than many built-in voices. It runs entirely on your device: nothing you read is sent anywhere.

        Before you turn it on
        • Downloads: about 20 MB the first time (the pronunciation dictionary and the speech engine), plus about 63 MB for each voice you choose, from jsDelivr and Hugging Face. Use Wi-Fi if your data is limited. Your browser keeps them, so they aren’t downloaded again unless you remove them or clear this site’s data.
        • Uses your device’s processor. Speech is made as you listen. On most computers and recent phones it’s made faster than it’s spoken. Older phones may pause briefly between sentences and use more battery.
        • English only, with British and American voices. Use a device voice for other languages.
        • Word highlighting is estimated, so it can run slightly ahead or behind the voice.
        • Openly licensed. The voices were trained on public-domain recordings. Pronunciation uses eSpeak NG, which is free software under the GPL 3.0 licence. See Licences for details and source code.

        You can switch back to a device voice at any time under Display, and remove the downloads there.

        Recognise text

        Before you start
        • Runs on your device. Velvet Scroll reads the page images itself with Tesseract, an open-source text recogniser. Nothing from your book is uploaded.
        • A one-time download of about 7 MB (English language data and the recognition engine) from jsDelivr. Your browser keeps it, so it isn’t downloaded again unless you clear this site’s data.
        • Takes a little while: a second or two per page on most computers, longer on phones. You can keep reading while it works and stop at any time. Finished pages are kept.
        • Works best on clear, printed English. Other languages in the Latin alphabet come out less accurately, handwriting isn’t recognised, and there may be some mistakes.
        • Openly licensed. Tesseract and Tesseract.js are free software under the Apache 2.0 licence. See Licences for details.

        The recognised text is saved in this browser with the book, so it’s ready next time. Removing the book removes it too.

        Backup and restore

        Save a backup

        One file with your books, covers, reading positions, shelves and settings. Keep it on a USB drive or in your own cloud storage, then restore it in Velvet Scroll on another computer or browser.

        With a password, the backup is encrypted with AES-256 and can't be opened without it. A forgotten password can't be recovered.

        Restore a backup

        Adds the books from a backup to this library. For books you already have, the most recent reading position and details are kept.

        Sync code

        Copy your reading places, shelves, book details and settings to another browser or device, with no account. The code is encrypted with a passphrase you choose. Books themselves aren’t included: copy them once with a backup, then use a sync code whenever you switch devices.
